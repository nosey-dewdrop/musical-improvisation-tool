The Four Chords That Rule Pop

the four chords that rule pop

Pop music has a public secret: an enormous part of it is built on one progression · I–V–vi–IV. In C major that is C → G → Am → F. The comedy group Axis of Awesome once played dozens of hits over this single loop, and the joke lands because it is true.

Why does it work?

It tells a complete emotional story in four bars: home (I), tension (V), sadness (vi), comfort (IV), and back home. Human ears never get tired of that round trip.

The siblings

I–vi–IV–V · the 50s doo-wop progression. "Stand By Me" made it immortal; every slow dance of that decade leaned on it.

vi–IV–I–V · the same four chords starting from the sad one. Instant melancholy; half of the 2010s radio pop starts here.

ii–V–I · jazz's heartbeat. "Autumn Leaves" is practically a ii–V–I étude. Learn to hear this one and jazz standards open like doors.

I–IV–V · the 12-bar blues skeleton, the grandfather of rock and roll.

i–VII–VI–V · the Andalusian cadence, ancient and dramatic. "Hit the Road Jack" walks down it the whole song.

How to use this site with them

Find your key on the detector, open the progressions page, and tap any of these · the site translates the numerals into real chords for your key instantly. Play the loop, then improvise over it using the scale notes. Congratulations: you are now the rhythm section and the soloist.
