Seven Modes, Seven Moods

seven modes, seven moods

Here is the most generous trick in music: take the seven notes of one major scale, change nothing, and just start from a different note. You get a completely different mood. These are the modes · seven moods hiding inside every key you find on this site.

Ionian · the major scale itself

Happy, bright, home. Most pop and folk lives here. "Let It Be" is pure Ionian comfort.

Dorian · minor with hope

Start from the 2nd note. It is minor, but with one raised note that adds a jazzy, hopeful shimmer. Miles Davis built "So What" on it; "Scarborough Fair" and Santana's "Oye Como Va" breathe it.

Phrygian · the Spanish shadow

Start from the 3rd note and the very first step is a half step · instantly dark, instantly Spanish. Flamenco lives here, and metal guitarists love it for riffs that feel dangerous.

Lydian · the dream

Start from the 4th note. One raised note makes everything float, like a film about flying. The Simpsons theme is the textbook Lydian melody.

Mixolydian · major with dirt

Start from the 5th note. It is major, but the softened 7th makes it bluesy and easy-going. "Sweet Home Alabama" and The Beatles' "Norwegian Wood" ride on it.

Aeolian · the natural minor

Start from the 6th note. This is the sad one, the emotional one. "Losing My Religion" stays in it from the first bar to the last.

Locrian · the ghost

Start from the 7th note and even the home chord is unstable (diminished). Music history almost never uses it · which tells you something: our ears vote, and Locrian keeps losing the election. It survives as spice, not as a home.

Practical use: find your key on the detector page, then click through the seven modes and listen to how the same notes change costume. Improvise in Dorian one day, Mixolydian the next · same fingers, new feelings.
